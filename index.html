What is it

What a BTC bridge does

Bitcoin cannot run Ethereum smart contracts, so BTC cannot be used directly in EVM DeFi. A BTC bridge locks native BTC on the Bitcoin network and issues a wrapped token on an EVM chain such as Ethereum or Arbitrum. Burning that token later releases native BTC back to a Bitcoin address.

Wrapped BTC tokens

The wrapped asset is an ERC-20 token that tracks the price of BTC. WBTC, tBTC and cbBTC are examples of different models; they are named here only as examples, and this interface has no partnership with any of them.

Who holds the BTC

Every bridge relies on some party or network that holds the locked BTC: a single custodian, a federation, or a threshold network of signers. Nothing on Bitcoin itself enforces the wrapped token on the EVM side. The interface only builds the transactions; it never holds your funds.

Custodial vs threshold

Custodial vs threshold-bridged BTC

The two common designs differ mainly in who controls the locked BTC and what you must trust to redeem it.

Custodial wrapped BTC vs threshold-bridged BTC
CustodialThreshold network
Who holds the BTCA company or custodian holds the keysA group of signers holds key shares; no single party holds the full key
MintingApproved through the custodian, often with identity checksDone through the protocol's contracts and signer network
RedemptionRequest to the custodian, who sends BTCBurn on the EVM side; the signer network releases BTC
Trust modelTrust one organization and its legal and operational controlsTrust that enough signers stay honest and the code is correct

No trustless option

Threshold designs spread the trust but do not remove it. Compare each token's reserves, signer set and redemption rules before you choose one.

How to bridge

How to bridge BTC to an EVM chain

Two wallets, two networks

You use a Bitcoin wallet to send BTC and an EVM wallet to receive the wrapped token. The interface shows the quote, the destination address and the expected output before anything is signed.

Step by step

  1. Choose the destination EVM chain and the wrapped BTC token you want to receive.
  2. Connect your EVM wallet and check that the receiving address is yours.
  3. Enter the amount of BTC and review the quote, fees and expected output.
  4. Sign and send the BTC transaction from your Bitcoin wallet to the deposit address shown.
  5. Wait for Bitcoin confirmations; the wrapped token is then minted or released to your EVM wallet.

To go back, burn or redeem the wrapped token on the EVM chain and give a Bitcoin address to receive native BTC.

Fees & gas

What bridging BTC costs

Bitcoin network fee

Sending BTC is a normal Bitcoin transaction, so you pay a network fee in BTC. It rises and falls with demand for block space, and low fees can mean slower confirmation.

EVM gas

On the EVM side, any approval, claim or redemption transaction is paid in the chain's native token: ETH on Ethereum and Arbitrum, or the native token of another chain. Holding wrapped BTC does not pay for gas.

Bridge or protocol fee

Some designs charge a mint or redeem fee, or take a spread. Any such cost appears in the quote, along with the amount you should receive. Keep a little native token on the EVM chain before you start.

Security

Staying safe when bridging BTC

Non-custodial interface

You connect your own wallets and sign every transaction yourself. The interface builds and displays transactions; it never holds your funds or private keys.

Bridge risk remains

The custodian, federation or signer network behind the wrapped token can fail, be compromised or freeze redemptions. Smart contract bugs are a separate risk, so exposure is never zero.

Before you sign

  • Verify the token contract address on the EVM chain; fake wrapped BTC tokens are common.
  • Double-check the Bitcoin deposit address and your receiving EVM address.
  • Try a small amount first, especially for a new route or token.
  • Read the redemption terms of the wrapped token before you hold a large balance.
FAQ

BTC Bridge FAQ

What is a BTC bridge?

A BTC bridge locks native Bitcoin with a custodian, federation or threshold signer network, and issues a wrapped BTC token on an EVM chain. You can burn the token later to get native BTC back.

Does a BTC bridge send my actual bitcoin to Ethereum?

No. Native BTC always stays on the Bitcoin network. What reaches Ethereum or Arbitrum is a token that represents BTC held by the bridge's custodian or signer network.

Who holds my BTC while I hold wrapped BTC?

The custodian, federation or threshold network behind the token holds it. This interface never holds your funds; it only helps you build and sign the transactions from your own wallets.

What is the difference between WBTC, tBTC and cbBTC?

They are examples of different models: some are issued by a custodian, others by a threshold network of signers. Each has its own reserves, redemption rules and trust assumptions. This interface has no partnership with any of them.

Which wallets and gas do I need to bridge BTC?

You need a Bitcoin wallet to send BTC and an EVM wallet to receive the wrapped token. You pay a Bitcoin network fee in BTC, plus gas in ETH or the chain's native token for EVM-side transactions.

Can I turn wrapped BTC back into native BTC?

Yes, through the redemption process of the token you hold. You burn or redeem it on the EVM chain and provide a Bitcoin address. Timing, fees and any limits depend on that token's design.
